About KISLHC

Respiratory diseases such as Asthma, Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease, Pneumonia and Tuberculosis remain major causes of illness and death among Kenyans with available statistics suggesting a rising burden of these diseases possibly as a result of increased urbanization and air pollution.

The International Scientific Lung Health Conference offers a platform for dissemination of the most updated information on the latest developments in research and clinical practice in the field of lung health and respiratory medicine. It provides stakeholders an opportunity to debate on controversial issues, challenges, and solutions; share experiences and to bring their personal contribution to the area.

What KISLHC Sets Out to Do

The conference aims to unite stakeholders to accelerate progress toward ending the TB epidemic by 2030 and improving overall lung health.

Reviewing achievements and identifying gaps in management of chronic lung diseases

Promoting the implementation and scaling up of new WHO-recommended tools in lung Health.

Mobilizing political and financial commitment to bridge funding gaps for lung health research, innovation, and care.

Fostering collaboration across different sectors (health, civil society, academia, private sector) and ensuring community engagement.

Addressing health inequities, human rights, and stigma associated with lung diseases.

Public Health interventions towards Universal Health Coverage

Highlights from KISLHC 2026

The 7th KISLHC met in Diani, Kwale County, from 5 to 8 August 2026 under the theme "From Detection to Recovery: Integrated Pathways and Patient-Centred Innovations in Lung Health", with pre-conference skills workshops, plenaries, oral and e-poster sessions, and the awards gala.

Frequently Asked Questions

General information about KISLHC 2027

Quick answers to the most common questions about the conference, registration, and abstracts.

KISLHC is a national and regional scientific meeting dedicated to advancing lung health through research, clinical practice, policy dialogue, and partnership. It brings together healthcare professionals, researchers, policymakers, and industry partners to share evidence, discuss challenges, and highlight practical solutions for respiratory health.

The conference also creates space for learning across disciplines, with sessions that connect prevention, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, and health system strengthening. In that sense, it is both a scientific forum and a collaboration platform for people working to improve lung health outcomes.

The convention is organized by the KISLHC planning committee, working with a network of stakeholders from government, professional societies, academic institutions, and health-sector partners. This collaborative approach helps ensure the programme reflects current priorities in lung health and brings together a wide range of expertise.

The organizing team is responsible for scientific content, logistics, communications, and participant support, while partner institutions contribute through expertise, sponsorship, publicity, and technical input. Together, they help deliver a conference that is scientifically strong, practical, and inclusive.

The convention is open to healthcare professionals, researchers, students, policymakers, development partners, public health practitioners, and other stakeholders interested in lung health. It is designed to be multidisciplinary, so people from different backgrounds can learn from one another and contribute meaningfully.

Whether you work in clinical care, public health programming, research, advocacy, education, or administration, you will find sessions relevant to your work. Students and early-career professionals are also encouraged to attend because the event provides a strong learning and networking environment.

Yes. County government employees working in health and related sectors are welcome to participate, subject to the usual approval and duty-release procedures in their institutions. The conference content is relevant to county-level service delivery, policy implementation, and health system strengthening.

Participation from county teams is especially valuable because many lung health priorities are implemented at county level. Attendance can therefore support knowledge exchange, planning, and alignment with national and local health goals.

When registration opens, you will register online with a ReSoK account, the same login used for abstract submission. Members of the Society use their existing portal login; everyone else creates a free account in a minute. You then choose the category that fits your role, such as delegate, student, or day participant.

Once your payment is confirmed you receive a confirmation email with a personal QR code. The same code is printed on your badge and scanned at the venue each day, which also records your attendance for CPD points.

Registration fees vary depending on the participant category, because the conference supports different audiences with different levels of access and support. The fee schedule includes categories for local delegates, regional and international participants, students, day-rate attendees, members, workshop participants, and organizing committee members.

The KISLHC 2027 fees will be published in the registration section when registration opens. If you are unsure which category applies to you, contact the secretariat before paying.

No. Abstracts are submitted only through the online abstract system, which opens on 1 November 2026 and closes on 31 May 2027 at 23:59 East Africa Time. This ensures every abstract is captured in the same format and reviewed blind.

Email is generally best reserved for support, technical questions, or follow-up communication with the abstracts team. If a portal or submission link is provided, it should be used as the primary submission route unless the secretariat gives different instructions.

No separate fee is charged for abstract submission. In other words, submitting an abstract is not treated as an extra paid service beyond the normal conference process.

However, if an abstract is accepted and the author wishes to attend the conference, standard registration fees may still apply depending on the participant category. It is always a good idea to check the registration information for the latest details.

Review starts after the deadline of 31 May 2027. Every author receives the decision by email, and can also see the status of each abstract at any time by signing in to the abstract system.

If you submit an abstract, it is important to monitor the inbox you used during registration as well as any conference announcements. That helps you avoid missing important updates about acceptance, revisions, or presentation instructions.

Yes. A single author may submit more than one abstract, provided each submission is distinct, scientifically sound, and follows the conference guidelines. This allows researchers and teams to share different pieces of work from separate studies or project components.

Each abstract should stand on its own and be submitted in the required format so that it can be reviewed independently. If your submissions are part of a larger project, make sure the focus, objectives, and findings are clearly different.

Yes. Organizations, partners, and companies can support the convention through sponsorship packages, exhibition opportunities, and broader partnership arrangements. Sponsorship is an important way to support the scientific programme while also increasing visibility for the sponsor’s brand or institution.

The conference welcomes support from institutions that share an interest in lung health, public health, innovation, and community impact. Sponsorship options usually vary in size and benefit, allowing organizations to choose a level of support that fits their goals and budget.

Interested organizations can contact the conference team through the sponsorship or contact pages to request the sponsor and exhibitor information. The team can then share details about available packages, exhibition space, branding opportunities, and any deadlines or requirements.

This is usually the best first step if your organization wants to compare the options before committing. It also gives the organizing team a chance to understand your goals and suggest the most suitable sponsorship or exhibitor category.

Service providers and suppliers interested in working with KISLHC can reach out through the official contact channels to express interest and request guidance on the appropriate engagement process. Depending on the type of service, the organizing team may direct you to procurement, sponsorship, or partnership contacts.

Doing this early is helpful because it allows the team to understand your offering and share any relevant requirements, timelines, or selection steps. Clear communication at the start also makes it easier to identify whether your organization fits the conference’s needs.

Yes. Approved exhibitors are allocated designated exhibition space as part of the exhibitor arrangement. The space is intended to support engagement with delegates, showcase products or services, and create visibility during the conference.

The exact setup usually depends on the package or agreement that has been confirmed with the organizing team. If space, location, or technical requirements matter to your organization, those details should be discussed early during the planning process.

Yes. Sponsors typically receive recognition and benefits based on the selected sponsorship package and the agreed partnership terms. These benefits may include branding visibility, acknowledgment during sessions, exhibition presence, and other forms of conference recognition.

The specific package determines the level of exposure and the type of benefits provided. Sponsors are encouraged to discuss their expectations with the organizing team so that the partnership aligns with both the conference needs and the sponsor’s objectives.

The best way to stay informed is to regularly check the official conference website and follow the conference’s communication channels. Those channels are used to share programme updates, speaker announcements, registration information, and any important changes.

If you are registered or have already interacted with the conference team, also keep an eye on your email inbox so you do not miss targeted notifications. Staying connected through official channels is the most reliable way to receive accurate information.

If your abstract will not submit, the problem is often related to incomplete fields, file format issues, slow connectivity, or a session timing out before the form is completed. The first step is to review the form carefully and make sure every required field has been filled in correctly.

If the issue persists, refresh the page, try a different browser, or resubmit after checking your internet connection. If none of those steps works, contact the abstracts support team with a clear description of the problem so they can help you troubleshoot it.

You can use the contact page or email the conference secretariat for help with registration, abstracts, sponsorship, exhibition, logistics, and general enquiries. That is the quickest route for getting official guidance from the organizing team.

When writing in, it helps to include your full name, organization, and a short description of your question so the team can respond more efficiently. Clear details usually make it easier to direct your enquiry to the right person.

The conference sub-themes cover the broad areas that shape lung health practice and policy, including prevention, early detection, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, health systems strengthening, and financing. They also reflect current priorities such as innovation, digital health, and integrated care across the life course.

These themes are intended to help delegates understand how different parts of the health system connect and how research can translate into better services for patients. They also provide a useful structure for organizing presentations, workshops, and discussions across the programme.
