Clinical Skills Workshops and Camps

Recent ReSoK practical workshops and camps strengthening early diagnosis, procedural access, clinical collaboration, and hands-on respiratory care across Kenyan health facilities.

EBUS and basic bronchoscopy training

Strengthening diagnostic and procedural skills at KNH

The EBUS and Basic Bronchoscopy Workshop at Kenyatta National Hospital was an intensive three-day training program designed to strengthen diagnostic and procedural capacity in lung health. The workshop combined expert-led teaching, live demonstrations, simulation-based learning, and structured case discussions.

Participants were introduced to foundational bronchoscopy skills, including airway anatomy, scope handling, bronchoalveolar lavage, biopsy techniques, and diagnostic sampling. The program also covered live EBUS procedures for lung cancer staging and lymph node evaluation, with simulation sessions focused on TBNA technique and troubleshooting.

As the workshop progressed, participants engaged with real-time diagnostic and therapeutic cases, including bronchoscopy for tuberculosis, foreign body removal, specimen handling, biosafety, and complex airway navigation. The final day focused on advanced scenarios, including sarcoidosis, complex EBUS cases, and emergency airway bleeding management.

By equipping healthcare professionals with these minimally invasive techniques, the workshop supports earlier diagnosis, safer procedures, shorter treatment timelines, and stronger respiratory care systems. ReSoK acknowledges the faculty, participants, partners, and sponsors who contributed to the success of the program.

Lung biopsy and multidisciplinary care

Building lung biopsy capacity in Mombasa

The three-day Lung Biopsy Workshop at Coast General Teaching and Referral Hospital in Mombasa provided practical training in essential respiratory and thoracic procedures, including bronchoscopy, pleuroscopy, and Video-Assisted Thoracoscopic Surgery.

The workshop responded to the growing burden of lung cancer and the need for earlier, more accurate diagnosis. Through hands-on training and multidisciplinary collaboration, participating healthcare professionals strengthened the procedural skills needed to investigate suspected lung disease and support timely treatment planning.

Alongside technical training, the workshop reinforced the value of multidisciplinary team approaches, patient education, and community awareness. These elements are central to reducing stigma, encouraging preventive action, and improving outcomes for people affected by lung cancer and other serious respiratory conditions.

ReSoK acknowledges the clinicians, partners, faculty, and institutional teams whose collaboration made the Mombasa workshop possible and helped advance access to quality lung health services.

Bronchoscopy and early lung disease diagnosis

Expanding specialised lung care in Kakamega

ReSoK concluded a two-day Bronchoscopy Camp at Kakamega County General Teaching and Referral Hospital, delivered as part of ongoing efforts to improve early diagnosis and treatment of lung disease. The camp focused on expanding access to bronchoscopy services for patients who require timely evaluation of complex respiratory symptoms.

During the camp, the clinical team performed bronchoscopic procedures to support diagnosis and management of conditions including suspected lung cancer, tuberculosis, chronic infections, and airway disorders. The activity also created a practical learning environment for healthcare providers, strengthening procedural confidence, referral pathways, and multidisciplinary coordination.

The Kakamega camp builds on previous outreach work in Mombasa and reflects ReSoK's commitment to bringing specialised respiratory services closer to communities. Through partnerships, continuous education, and local capacity building, these camps support earlier detection, reduced stigma, and improved access to essential lung care.

Bronchoscopy, VATS, and access to specialist care

Bringing advanced lung care closer to Meru County

During Lung Cancer, COPD, and Pulmonary Hypertension Awareness Month, ReSoK supported a two-day free Bronchoscopy and VATS Camp at Meru Teaching and Referral Hospital. The camp was designed to improve access to specialised respiratory and thoracic services for patients who might otherwise face delays in diagnosis and treatment.

Over the two days, clinical teams performed bronchoscopic and thoracoscopic procedures to support timely diagnosis and management of lung cancer, COPD, tuberculosis, chronic infections, pulmonary hypertension, and airway disorders. For many patients, the camp created a first opportunity to access specialised lung care closer to home.

Bronchoscopy allows clinicians to directly examine the airways and obtain tissue samples from within the lungs, supporting diagnosis where imaging alone may not be sufficient. VATS, or Video-Assisted Thoracoscopic Surgery, uses small incisions and a camera-assisted approach to diagnose and treat lung diseases with less pain, faster recovery, and fewer complications than open surgery.

The Meru camp built on previous outreach work in Mombasa and Kakamega, reinforcing ReSoK's commitment to extending specialist respiratory care across counties through collaboration, training, and patient-centred service delivery.

Pleural care and procedural training

Advancing access to minimally invasive pleural procedures

"The good physician treats the disease; the great physician treats the patient who has the disease." - William Osler

The Two-Day Pleuroscopy Camp marked an important step in strengthening respiratory care and expanding access to specialised pleural disease services. The initiative brought together skilled clinicians, nurses, and support teams to deliver focused diagnostic and therapeutic interventions for patients with pleural disease.

Through minimally invasive pleuroscopy procedures, the teams supported more accurate diagnosis and timely management of conditions such as unexplained pleural effusions and malignancies. The camp combined patient care with hands-on learning, reinforcing safe procedural practice, multidisciplinary coordination, and careful post-procedure follow-up.

Beyond the procedures performed, the activity contributed to early detection, improved patient outcomes, and strengthened local capacity in respiratory medicine. ReSoK acknowledges the healthcare professionals, partners, support teams, and patients whose participation made the camp possible.
