EBUS and basic bronchoscopy training

Advancing Lung Health Through Skills: Inside the EBUS and Basic Bronchoscopy Workshop at Kenyatta National Hospital

When it comes to lung health, the difference between uncertainty and a clear diagnosis can often come down to having the right expertise, technology, and confidence to look deeper.

This was at the heart of the EBUS and Basic Bronchoscopy Workshop at Kenyatta National Hospital, an intensive three-day learning experience focused on equipping healthcare professionals with practical knowledge and techniques for the diagnosis and management of respiratory conditions using modern, minimally invasive approaches.

Through expert-led teaching, live demonstrations, simulation-based practice, and structured clinical case discussions, the workshop created an environment where participants could move beyond theory and engage directly with the realities of respiratory procedures. It was a space to learn, practise, ask questions, troubleshoot challenges, and build the confidence needed to translate new knowledge into patient care.

At its core, the programme reflected a simple but important principle: improving outcomes for people living with lung disease begins with ensuring that healthcare professionals have the knowledge and tools to recognize disease early, investigate it accurately, and make informed decisions about treatment.

Moving Beyond the Basics of Bronchoscopy

Bronchoscopy gives clinicians a direct view of the airways and plays an important role in investigating a wide range of respiratory conditions. However, performing the procedure effectively requires more than familiarity with the equipment. It demands a clear understanding of anatomy, careful technique, sound clinical judgement, and attention to patient safety.

The workshop began with these essential foundations.

Participants explored airway anatomy and the principles of bronchoscopy before progressing to practical aspects of scope handling and navigation. They were introduced to procedures including bronchoalveolar lavage, biopsy techniques, and diagnostic specimen collection, with attention to how each stage contributes to obtaining reliable clinical information.

These technical steps have a direct bearing on patient care. The quality of a specimen, the precision with which it is obtained, and the way it is handled can all influence the accuracy of a diagnosis and the treatment decisions that follow.

By grounding participants in these fundamentals, the workshop provided a strong platform for progressing into more advanced procedures and clinical scenarios.

Bringing Learning Into the Procedure Room

One of the most valuable aspects of the workshop was the opportunity to observe techniques being applied in real clinical situations.

Live procedures offered participants a practical view of how endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) can be used to evaluate lymph nodes and support the staging of lung cancer. For clinicians, observing the procedure in practice provided an important connection between what is taught in theory and what happens at the bedside.

EBUS is an important development in respiratory medicine, offering a minimally invasive way of investigating structures around the airways and obtaining samples that can help guide diagnosis and treatment.

The workshop complemented these live demonstrations with simulation-based learning. Participants had the opportunity to practise transbronchial needle aspiration (TBNA) techniques in a controlled setting, allowing them to repeat procedures, refine their approach, troubleshoot difficulties, and develop greater confidence before encountering similar situations in clinical practice.

This blend of observation and hands-on learning was central to the programme. Participants were not simply shown how a procedure works; they were encouraged to understand the reasoning behind each step and to engage with the practical challenges that can arise during respiratory interventions.

Learning Through Real Clinical Challenges

Respiratory medicine rarely follows a perfectly predictable script.

Patients may present with overlapping symptoms, unusual findings, difficult anatomy, or complex diagnostic questions. For clinicians, this means that procedural knowledge must be accompanied by adaptability, problem-solving, and the ability to make sound decisions in real time.

The workshop brought this reality into the learning environment through discussions and demonstrations centred on clinical cases.

Participants engaged with scenarios involving tuberculosis, foreign body removal, specimen handling, biosafety, and complex airway navigation. These cases encouraged consideration of not only how a procedure is performed, but also why a particular approach may be appropriate for a specific patient.

The focus on tuberculosis was especially relevant to respiratory care in settings where the disease remains an important health concern. Obtaining appropriate specimens safely and effectively is an important part of establishing an accurate diagnosis and ensuring that patients can proceed to appropriate treatment.

Attention to biosafety further reinforced the responsibility that accompanies respiratory procedures. Protecting patients and healthcare workers requires careful adherence to infection prevention measures, appropriate specimen handling, and a clear understanding of the risks associated with diagnostic procedures.

In this way, the workshop connected technical proficiency with the broader principles of safe, high-quality clinical care.

From Skill-Building to Advanced Clinical Thinking

As the programme progressed, participants moved from foundational techniques into increasingly complex scenarios requiring greater clinical judgement.

Advanced sessions explored cases involving sarcoidosis and complex EBUS procedures, giving participants an opportunity to consider the challenges that arise when respiratory disease does not present in straightforward ways.

The programme also addressed emergency airway bleeding management, an important area of preparation for anyone performing bronchoscopic procedures. Although complications may be uncommon, knowing how to recognize and respond to them can make a critical difference when they occur.

These sessions reinforced an important lesson: successful bronchoscopy is not simply about mastering the mechanics of a procedure. It also involves knowing when and why to intervene, understanding the limitations of a technique, anticipating potential complications, and responding appropriately when unexpected situations arise.

That combination of technical ability and clinical judgement is what ultimately makes specialized respiratory care safer and more effective.

Growing a Stronger Respiratory Workforce

At its core, the workshop was about people - the healthcare professionals who stand between advances in respiratory medicine and the patients who need them.

Technology can open new possibilities for diagnosis and treatment, but its value depends on the people who know how to use it. Creating opportunities for clinicians to learn from experienced faculty, practise new techniques, and share experiences helps ensure that advances in respiratory medicine can be translated into everyday care.

A clinician who gains confidence in bronchoscopy and EBUS can use that knowledge not only in individual patient encounters, but also through mentorship, teamwork, and knowledge-sharing with colleagues.

This is closely aligned with the work of the Respiratory Society of Kenya (ReSoK) and its commitment to advancing lung health through education, training, research, and professional collaboration.

By bringing respiratory professionals together around practical learning and shared clinical experiences, initiatives such as this help nurture a community of practitioners who can continue learning from one another while contributing to better respiratory care across Kenya.

Putting Patients at the Centre

Behind every bronchoscopy procedure is a patient waiting for answers.

It may be someone with persistent respiratory symptoms. Someone undergoing investigation for suspected lung cancer. Someone requiring further evaluation for tuberculosis or sarcoidosis. Or someone whose diagnosis remains uncertain and whose treatment depends on obtaining the right information.

For such patients, access to appropriately trained professionals and modern diagnostic techniques can make a meaningful difference.

A timely and accurate procedure can help clarify a diagnosis. A good-quality specimen can provide the information needed to guide treatment. A minimally invasive approach can reduce the burden associated with more invasive investigations. And a clinician who is confident in managing potential complications can help ensure that the procedure is performed as safely as possible.

Ultimately, the value of a workshop such as this extends beyond the classroom and procedure room. Its greatest significance lies in what participants are able to take back to their hospitals, their teams, and, most importantly, their patients.

Looking Ahead for Lung Health in Kenya

The EBUS and Basic Bronchoscopy Workshop at Kenyatta National Hospital speaks to a broader need within respiratory medicine: ensuring that healthcare professionals are able to keep pace with advances in diagnosis while responding to the realities of the patients they serve.

Lung diseases are diverse, and their diagnosis can be complex. Meeting these challenges requires continuous learning, collaboration, research, and access to appropriate technologies and expertise.

This is where the role of professional societies such as ReSoK becomes particularly important. By creating spaces for learning, professional exchange, research, and mentorship, ReSoK contributes to a respiratory community that is better prepared to respond to the changing needs of patients and the health system.

The workshop also highlighted the value of collaboration. Faculty, participants, institutions, partners, and sponsors each played a role in creating an environment where knowledge could be shared and practical skills developed.

Their collective contribution reflects what is possible when professionals and organizations come together around a common purpose: improving the quality of lung health care and ensuring that patients have access to timely, safe, and effective respiratory services.

The EBUS and Basic Bronchoscopy Workshop was therefore more than a three-day programme of lectures, demonstrations, simulations, and case discussions. It was an opportunity to learn from experience, practise new techniques, exchange knowledge, and strengthen confidence in caring for patients with complex respiratory conditions.

For ReSoK, such initiatives are part of a continuing journey - one that places knowledge, collaboration, research, and patient-centred care at the heart of advancing lung health in Kenya.

And as more healthcare professionals gain exposure to modern diagnostic techniques and share their knowledge with others, the impact reaches far beyond the workshop itself: into hospitals, into clinical practice, and ultimately into the lives of the patients whose search for answers begins with a simple but important question - what is happening inside my lungs?

Lung biopsy and multidisciplinary care

Expanding the Possibilities of Lung Diagnosis: Inside the Lung Biopsy Workshop at Coast General Teaching and Referral Hospital

A diagnosis can change everything.

For a patient with a persistent cough, unexplained chest symptoms, or a suspicious finding on imaging, the journey from uncertainty to treatment often depends on one crucial step: finding out exactly what is happening inside the lungs.

This was the focus of the three-day Lung Biopsy Workshop at Coast General Teaching and Referral Hospital in Mombasa, which brought healthcare professionals together for practical learning in a range of respiratory and thoracic procedures used to investigate suspected lung disease.

The workshop provided an opportunity to explore bronchoscopy, pleuroscopy, and Video-Assisted Thoracoscopic Surgery (VATS) through hands-on training and multidisciplinary engagement. More than simply introducing participants to individual procedures, the programme demonstrated how different approaches can work together to answer difficult diagnostic questions and inform the next steps in patient care.

At a time when lung cancer continues to present significant challenges for patients and health systems, strengthening the ability to investigate suspected disease accurately and efficiently is increasingly important.

Looking Deeper Into Lung Disease

Diagnosing lung disease is not always straightforward.

Symptoms can overlap across different respiratory conditions, while abnormalities identified through imaging may require further investigation before clinicians can determine their significance. In suspected lung cancer, obtaining an appropriate tissue sample can be an essential part of establishing a definitive diagnosis and guiding treatment.

This is where lung biopsy and related procedures become particularly important.

The workshop introduced participants to techniques that allow clinicians to approach different areas of the chest and lungs depending on the clinical question at hand. Bronchoscopy provides access through the airways, while pleuroscopy enables examination of the pleural space. VATS offers a minimally invasive surgical approach that can provide access to areas that may not be reachable through conventional techniques.

Understanding the role of each procedure - and knowing when it may be appropriate - forms an important part of modern respiratory and thoracic practice.

For healthcare professionals, practical exposure to these techniques offers an opportunity to develop greater familiarity with the procedures, the equipment involved, and the clinical considerations that accompany them.

Learning Through Hands-On Experience

One of the defining features of the Mombasa workshop was its practical approach.

Rather than limiting learning to lectures and theoretical discussions, participants engaged directly with procedural techniques. This allowed them to move through the different stages of respiratory and thoracic interventions while learning from experienced faculty and colleagues.

Hands-on training can provide a different kind of understanding. It allows healthcare professionals to appreciate the practical realities of positioning, navigation, instrumentation, sampling, and procedural decision-making - details that can be difficult to fully capture in a presentation.

It also creates room for questions and discussion around situations that clinicians encounter in their own practice.

For procedures as technically demanding as bronchoscopy, pleuroscopy, and VATS, that practical exposure can help bridge the gap between knowing how a procedure works and understanding how it is applied in the clinical environment.

Responding to the Lung Cancer Challenge

The relevance of this training extends beyond the procedures themselves.

Lung cancer can be difficult to detect early because its symptoms may be mistaken for other respiratory conditions, particularly when patients present late or face barriers to accessing specialized care. Once a suspicious lesion is identified, however, timely investigation becomes an important part of determining what comes next.

Accurate tissue diagnosis can help clinicians understand the nature of a lesion and inform decisions around treatment.

This makes the availability of appropriate diagnostic expertise particularly important. Where specialized procedures are accessible and clinicians are familiar with their use, patients may have a clearer pathway from suspicion to diagnosis and, ultimately, treatment planning.

The Mombasa workshop contributed to that conversation by bringing attention to the practical skills and collaborative approaches required to investigate complex thoracic conditions.

Its setting at Coast General Teaching and Referral Hospital also provided an important context for learning within a major referral environment, where clinicians encounter a diverse range of respiratory and thoracic presentations.

When Different Disciplines Come Together

Lung disease does not fit neatly into the boundaries of a single specialty.

A patient may require the expertise of a pulmonologist, thoracic surgeon, radiologist, pathologist, anaesthetist, oncologist, nurse, or other healthcare professional at different stages of the diagnostic and treatment journey.

The workshop therefore emphasized the importance of multidisciplinary collaboration.

Bringing different areas of expertise together allows clinical decisions to be considered from multiple perspectives. Imaging findings can be discussed alongside procedural options. Pathological information can be interpreted in the context of the patient's clinical presentation. Surgical and non-surgical approaches can be considered together.

For patients, this can translate into more coordinated decision-making and a clearer understanding of the available options.

The multidisciplinary approach also reinforces an important reality of modern lung health: improving outcomes is rarely the responsibility of one professional working alone. It is the result of different skills and perspectives coming together around the needs of the patient.

Beyond the Procedure

While technical expertise formed a major part of the workshop, the conversations around lung disease extended beyond the procedure room.

Patient education and community awareness remain important components of improving outcomes, particularly for conditions such as lung cancer that can be surrounded by fear, misconceptions, and stigma.

A patient who understands why a biopsy is being recommended may be better positioned to participate in decisions about their care. Similarly, communities that are more aware of respiratory symptoms and potential risk factors may be more likely to seek medical attention when concerns arise.

Reducing stigma also matters.

Conversations about lung cancer and other serious respiratory conditions can sometimes be shaped by assumptions about blame, lifestyle, or personal responsibility. Creating space for accurate information and open dialogue can help shift the focus towards early recognition, appropriate care, and support for people affected by disease.

In this sense, improving lung health extends beyond what happens inside a hospital. It also involves how communities understand respiratory disease and how confidently people seek care when they need it.

Putting Knowledge to Work for Patients

At the centre of every diagnostic procedure is a person waiting for answers.

For someone with a suspicious lung lesion, the uncertainty can be overwhelming. Questions about what the finding means, whether it is cancer, what treatment might be required, and what the future holds can weigh heavily on patients and their families.

A well-performed diagnostic procedure cannot remove all of that uncertainty, but it can help replace speculation with evidence.

The ability to obtain an appropriate sample, interpret the findings accurately, and bring the right specialists together around the results can help move a patient forward.

That is ultimately where the importance of workshops such as this lies. The techniques demonstrated and practised are not simply procedural skills; they are tools that can help clinicians answer important questions and guide the next stage of a patient's care.

Advancing Lung Health Through Collaboration

The Lung Biopsy Workshop in Mombasa reflects the broader importance of continuous professional learning in respiratory and thoracic medicine.

As diagnostic technologies and treatment approaches continue to evolve, healthcare professionals need opportunities to remain familiar with emerging techniques while sharing experiences from clinical practice.

For the Respiratory Society of Kenya (ReSoK), supporting education, training, research, and technical collaboration is part of a wider commitment to promoting and improving lung health in Kenya and beyond.

The Mombasa workshop brought that spirit to life by creating an environment where clinicians could learn together, exchange knowledge, and engage with procedures that have an important role in the diagnosis of serious respiratory conditions.

The contribution of the faculty, clinicians, partners, and institutional teams involved was central to making the programme possible. Their collaboration demonstrated what can happen when expertise and resources are brought together with a shared focus on better respiratory care.

The impact of such an initiative does not end when the workshop closes. The knowledge gained travels with participants back into their clinical environments, where it can inform practice, support colleagues, and ultimately benefit patients.

From the procedure room in Mombasa to the wider communities that depend on quality respiratory services, every step towards better diagnosis brings lung health closer to where it needs to be: at the centre of timely, informed, and patient-focused care.

Bringing Bronchoscopy Closer to Patients: The Kakamega Bronchoscopy Camp

For a patient living with unexplained respiratory symptoms, getting the right diagnosis can sometimes feel like a long journey.

Persistent cough, difficulty breathing, recurrent infections, or an abnormal finding on a scan can raise important questions that cannot always be answered through routine investigations alone. In some cases, clinicians need to look deeper - to examine the airways, obtain appropriate samples, and gather the information needed to understand what is affecting the patient.

It is in these moments that access to specialized respiratory services can make a meaningful difference.

This was the focus of the two-day Bronchoscopy Camp at Kakamega County General Teaching and Referral Hospital, where ReSoK brought specialized respiratory expertise closer to patients requiring further evaluation of complex lung conditions.

The camp formed part of ReSoK's continued efforts to support earlier diagnosis and appropriate management of lung disease while creating opportunities for healthcare professionals to learn, collaborate, and strengthen the delivery of respiratory care within their own settings.

Bringing Specialized Care Closer

Access to specialized procedures is an important part of the lung health journey.

For patients who require bronchoscopy, reaching a facility where the procedure is available can be an additional challenge. Distance, referral processes, costs, and limited access to specialized services can all influence how quickly a patient moves from suspicion to diagnosis.

The Kakamega Bronchoscopy Camp sought to bring that specialized service closer to the patients who needed it.

By conducting procedures at Kakamega County General Teaching and Referral Hospital, the camp created an opportunity for patients requiring further respiratory investigation to receive specialized attention within a regional referral setting.

This approach is consistent with a patient-centred vision of lung health - one in which quality respiratory services are not limited to a small number of locations, but progressively become more accessible to communities across the country.

Looking Deeper for Answers

Bronchoscopy allows clinicians to examine the airways directly and, where necessary, obtain specimens for further investigation.

During the camp, the clinical team performed bronchoscopic procedures supporting the diagnosis and management of conditions including suspected lung cancer, tuberculosis, chronic infections, and airway disorders.

Each patient presented a different clinical question.

For some, the procedure could help investigate a suspicious finding and determine whether further evaluation for lung cancer was necessary. For others, obtaining an appropriate respiratory specimen could contribute to the investigation of tuberculosis or another infection. In cases involving airway disorders, bronchoscopy could provide clinicians with a closer look at structures that cannot be fully assessed through symptoms and routine examinations alone.

The value of the procedure lies not simply in seeing the airways, but in the information it can provide.

A clearer picture can help clinicians determine what happens next - whether that means additional investigations, specialist review, or treatment planning.

More Than a Procedure

Although the bronchoscopy procedures were at the centre of the camp, the experience extended beyond the procedure room.

The camp also provided a practical learning environment for healthcare providers, allowing them to engage with the clinical team and gain greater familiarity with bronchoscopic procedures and patient management.

Learning in a real clinical setting offers an important perspective. Healthcare providers can observe how patients are assessed, how procedural decisions are made, how samples are obtained and handled, and how different professionals contribute to the patient's care.

These experiences can help build confidence while encouraging knowledge-sharing that continues after the camp has ended.

The programme also highlighted the importance of effective referral pathways and multidisciplinary coordination. A bronchoscopy does not exist in isolation; its findings may need to be interpreted alongside imaging, laboratory results, pathology, clinical history, and specialist assessment.

When these different parts of the healthcare system work together, the patient's journey becomes more connected.

The Importance of Earlier Diagnosis

Time matters in lung disease.

The longer a serious respiratory condition remains undiagnosed, the greater the potential for delays in starting appropriate care. This is particularly important when investigating conditions such as lung cancer and tuberculosis, where timely diagnosis can influence subsequent management.

For patients, however, early diagnosis is not simply a clinical objective. It can mean replacing uncertainty with answers.

It can mean understanding what is causing persistent symptoms, knowing which treatment options are available, and being able to make informed decisions about the next stage of care.

The Kakamega camp therefore formed part of a wider effort to make that journey more responsive - helping patients access the investigations they require while supporting healthcare teams to work collaboratively around their needs.

Connecting Expertise With Local Care

One of the strengths of outreach initiatives such as the Kakamega Bronchoscopy Camp is the opportunity to connect specialized expertise with healthcare providers working closer to communities.

The camp builds on ReSoK's previous outreach work, including activities in Mombasa, and reflects an approach that recognizes the importance of bringing expertise into local health systems rather than expecting every patient to travel long distances in search of specialized care.

This also creates opportunities for professionals from different settings to learn from one another.

Specialized respiratory teams bring procedural experience, while local healthcare providers bring an understanding of the patients, referral patterns, and challenges within their communities. When these perspectives meet, the result can be a more practical and context-sensitive approach to respiratory care.

Addressing More Than Disease

Improving lung health also requires addressing the factors that influence when and how people seek care.

Stigma surrounding conditions such as tuberculosis and lung cancer can discourage individuals from seeking medical attention or speaking openly about their symptoms. Misconceptions about respiratory disease can further contribute to delayed presentation and missed opportunities for early intervention.

This makes awareness and education an important part of the wider lung health agenda.

By supporting healthcare professionals and engaging with communities, initiatives such as the Kakamega camp contribute to a broader understanding that respiratory symptoms deserve attention and that people living with lung disease should be able to seek care without fear or stigma.

Early diagnosis begins with access to services, but it also begins with recognizing when something is wrong and having the confidence to seek help.

Keeping the Patient at the Centre

Behind every procedure is a person.

Someone who has been living with symptoms. Someone waiting to understand the cause of an abnormal test result. Someone whose family is anxious for answers. Someone hoping that the next investigation will finally provide clarity.

For these patients, bringing bronchoscopy services closer can remove one more barrier from an already challenging journey.

The Kakamega camp provided an avenue for specialized assessment while bringing clinicians together around the shared goal of improving patient care. It demonstrated how targeted clinical outreach, professional collaboration, and knowledge-sharing can work together to make specialized respiratory services more accessible.

Continuing the Journey

The Kakamega Bronchoscopy Camp is part of a continuing journey towards stronger and more accessible lung health services in Kenya.

For the Respiratory Society of Kenya (ReSoK), promoting and improving lung health requires more than awareness alone. It calls for continued research, education, training, technical support, and collaboration that can translate knowledge into meaningful improvements in patient care.

The Kakamega experience brought these elements together: specialized procedures for patients, practical learning for healthcare providers, collaboration across teams, and a focus on strengthening the connection between referral services and the communities they serve.

Its significance lies not only in the procedures performed over two days, but in the possibilities created beyond them.

A healthcare provider who gains greater confidence can support the next patient. A stronger referral connection can shorten the path to specialist care. A timely investigation can bring a patient closer to a diagnosis. And a diagnosis can open the door to treatment.

From Kakamega to other parts of the country, the goal remains clear: to make quality lung health care more accessible, more timely, and more responsive to the people who need it.

Bronchoscopy, VATS, and access to specialist care

Bringing Specialist Lung Care Closer: The Meru Bronchoscopy and VATS Camp

A diagnosis can be the beginning of treatment, but getting to that diagnosis is not always a simple journey.

For people living with persistent respiratory symptoms, suspected lung cancer, chronic lung disease, or other complex conditions, access to specialized investigations can determine how quickly they receive the answers and care they need.

During Lung Cancer, COPD, and Pulmonary Hypertension Awareness Month, the Respiratory Society of Kenya (ReSoK) supported a two-day free Bronchoscopy and Video-Assisted Thoracoscopic Surgery (VATS) Camp at Meru Teaching and Referral Hospital, bringing specialized respiratory and thoracic services closer to patients in need.

The camp provided an opportunity for patients who might otherwise experience delays in accessing specialist investigations to receive assessment and procedures closer to home. At the same time, it brought healthcare professionals together around practical clinical experience, collaboration, and patient-centred care.

The initiative reflected an important part of ReSoK's wider commitment to promoting and improving lung health in Kenya and beyond - ensuring that advances in respiratory medicine can reach the people and communities who need them.

When Answers Require a Closer Look

Respiratory disease does not always reveal itself clearly.

A patient may have symptoms that persist despite treatment. An imaging scan may show an abnormality, but leave important questions unanswered. A suspected tumour may require tissue confirmation. An airway problem may need direct visualization before clinicians can determine the most appropriate course of action.

In such situations, specialized procedures can provide information that routine investigations alone may not.

This is where bronchoscopy becomes particularly valuable.

During bronchoscopy, clinicians can use a flexible camera to examine the airways directly and, where necessary, obtain samples for laboratory and pathological examination. These samples can help investigate conditions such as lung cancer, tuberculosis, chronic infections, and other airway disorders.

For a patient waiting for an explanation for persistent or worrying symptoms, obtaining that information can represent an important step forward.

A Closer Look at the Lungs

The Meru camp also featured Video-Assisted Thoracoscopic Surgery (VATS), a minimally invasive approach used in the diagnosis and treatment of a range of thoracic conditions.

Unlike traditional open surgery, VATS uses small incisions through which a camera and surgical instruments can be introduced into the chest. This allows clinicians to examine and, where appropriate, treat disease while avoiding the larger incision associated with conventional open procedures.

For suitable patients, minimally invasive approaches can offer advantages such as less postoperative discomfort and a potentially faster recovery compared with open surgery.

The inclusion of both bronchoscopy and VATS allowed the clinical teams to approach respiratory and thoracic conditions using techniques suited to different diagnostic and therapeutic needs.

The procedures were not simply about technology. They were about using the right tools to answer the right clinical questions - and helping patients move forward with greater certainty.

From Suspicion to Diagnosis

For many people, the hardest part of dealing with a serious illness can be the uncertainty that comes before a diagnosis.

A persistent cough may raise questions about tuberculosis. An abnormal scan may prompt investigation for lung cancer. Long-standing respiratory symptoms may require further assessment for chronic lung disease. A patient may know that something is wrong without knowing exactly what it is.

That period of uncertainty can be difficult for both patients and their families.

The Meru camp created an opportunity for patients requiring specialized investigation to access these services closer to home. Over the two days, clinical teams performed bronchoscopic and thoracoscopic procedures supporting the diagnosis and management of lung cancer, COPD, tuberculosis, chronic infections, pulmonary hypertension, and airway disorders.

For some patients, it represented an important first encounter with specialized lung care.

For others, it offered another step in an ongoing diagnostic journey.

In both cases, the objective remained the same: helping clinicians gather the information needed to guide appropriate care.

Why Awareness Matters

Holding the camp during Lung Cancer, COPD, and Pulmonary Hypertension Awareness Month added another dimension to the initiative.

Awareness months provide an opportunity to move conversations about disease beyond clinical spaces and into the wider community. They create room to talk about symptoms, risk factors, prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and the importance of seeking care.

This is particularly important for lung diseases, which can sometimes be overlooked until symptoms become severe.

Lung cancer, for example, may initially present with symptoms that are easily attributed to other conditions. COPD can progressively affect a person's ability to breathe and carry out everyday activities. Pulmonary hypertension can also be difficult to recognize because some of its symptoms overlap with other cardiovascular and respiratory conditions.

Creating greater awareness can encourage people to take persistent symptoms seriously and seek appropriate medical attention.

But awareness must be accompanied by access.

Knowing that one should seek care means little if the necessary services remain difficult to reach. This is why initiatives that connect awareness with specialized clinical services can play an important role in the broader lung health agenda.

Bringing Care Closer to Home

For patients outside major urban centres, accessing specialist respiratory and thoracic services can involve additional challenges.

Travel can be costly and time-consuming. Referral processes can take time. Families may need to make arrangements around work, school, caregiving, and other responsibilities.

Bringing specialist services to a regional referral hospital can help reduce some of these barriers.

The Meru camp provided patients with an opportunity to access specialized procedures within their own region, rather than making the journey to distant facilities solely to seek specialist assessment.

This approach also strengthens the connection between specialist teams and healthcare providers working within local facilities. It creates opportunities to share knowledge, discuss clinical cases, and build relationships that can continue beyond the outreach activity.

Learning Alongside Service

While patients remained at the centre of the camp, the experience also created opportunities for healthcare professionals to learn from one another.

Specialized procedures such as bronchoscopy and VATS require technical expertise, clinical judgement, teamwork, and familiarity with patient safety considerations. Exposure to experienced teams in a practical environment can provide valuable opportunities for observation, discussion, and professional development.

The interaction between visiting specialists and local healthcare teams can also contribute to stronger referral and communication pathways.

A patient does not experience healthcare as a collection of separate departments. Their journey may move from a primary healthcare facility to a county hospital, then to specialist services and back again.

The smoother those connections become, the easier it can be to keep the patient's care moving in the right direction.

Part of a Wider Journey

The Meru camp did not stand alone.

It built on previous outreach activities in Mombasa and Kakamega, forming part of a broader effort to extend specialized respiratory services across different parts of the country.

Each setting brings its own realities, patient needs, healthcare structures, and challenges. Yet the underlying objective remains consistent: making quality lung health services more accessible while supporting healthcare professionals through education, training, collaboration, and shared expertise.

For ReSoK, this work reflects the belief that improving lung health requires action at multiple levels.

It means supporting professionals with the knowledge and skills they need. It means encouraging research and the exchange of evidence. It means working with partners and institutions to strengthen services. And it means keeping the patient at the centre of every intervention.

The Person Behind the Procedure

Every procedure performed during the camp represented more than a clinical intervention.

Behind each patient was a story.

A person who may have spent weeks or months living with symptoms. A family waiting for answers. A clinician looking for evidence to confirm a diagnosis. A treatment decision waiting on the results of an investigation.

For these individuals, access to specialized care can mean more than convenience. It can mean progress.

Progress from suspicion to diagnosis.

From uncertainty to understanding.

And, where appropriate, from diagnosis to treatment.

This is why bringing specialized services closer to communities matters.

Continuing to Advance Lung Health

The Meru Bronchoscopy and VATS Camp demonstrated what can happen when clinical expertise, institutional collaboration, professional learning, and patient-centred service delivery come together.

For ReSoK, such initiatives are part of a broader commitment to promoting and improving lung health through research, education, training, and technical support.

The contribution of the healthcare professionals, institutional teams, partners, and everyone involved in making the camp possible was central to its success.

More importantly, the experience reinforced a message that extends beyond Meru: quality lung health care should not be defined only by where a patient lives.

As ReSoK continues to collaborate with healthcare professionals and institutions across the country, the aim remains to help create a healthcare environment where people can access appropriate respiratory services, receive timely diagnoses, and move towards the care they need.

Because sometimes, bringing the right expertise closer to a patient is the first step towards bringing them closer to an answer.

Advancing Access to Minimally Invasive Pleural Procedures

"The good physician treats the disease; the great physician treats the patient who has the disease." - William Osler

A patient with fluid accumulating around the lungs may arrive with something as seemingly simple as shortness of breath or persistent chest discomfort. Yet behind those symptoms can lie a range of conditions - from infection and tuberculosis to malignancy - each requiring careful investigation before the right treatment can begin.

Finding the cause is therefore an important part of caring for a patient with pleural disease.

This was the focus of the Two-Day Pleuroscopy Camp, an initiative that brought together clinicians, nurses, and support teams to provide specialized pleural procedures while creating an environment for practical learning and professional collaboration.

At the centre of the camp was pleuroscopy, a minimally invasive technique that allows clinicians to examine the pleural space directly and, when necessary, obtain tissue samples to help establish a diagnosis.

But the value of the camp extended beyond the procedure itself. It brought together clinical expertise, technology, teamwork, and patient care around a common goal: helping people with pleural disease move more quickly from unexplained symptoms to answers and appropriate care.

When Breathing Problems Need a Closer Look

The pleura - the thin layers of tissue surrounding the lungs - can be affected by a number of diseases.

One of the most common clinical challenges is an unexplained pleural effusion, where excess fluid collects around the lung. Although a pleural effusion can have several causes, determining exactly why the fluid has accumulated is essential to deciding how the patient should be managed.

Sometimes routine investigations provide the answer. In other cases, they do not.

This is where more specialized procedures can become important.

Pleuroscopy allows clinicians to look directly into the pleural space and identify abnormalities that may not be adequately characterized through other investigations. It can also enable targeted biopsies, providing tissue that can be examined to help establish the underlying cause of disease.

For patients and families waiting for a diagnosis, that additional information can be crucial.

Seeing Beyond the Surface

One of the strengths of pleuroscopy is its ability to bring clinicians closer to the source of a patient's problem.

Instead of relying solely on symptoms or imaging findings, clinicians can directly assess the pleural space and identify areas that require further investigation. Where appropriate, tissue samples can then be obtained for diagnostic evaluation.

This can be particularly important when malignancy is suspected.

A diagnosis of cancer carries enormous implications for a patient. Before treatment decisions can be made, clinicians need reliable information about what they are dealing with. Obtaining an appropriate tissue sample is therefore not simply another step in an investigation - it can be the step that provides the clarity needed to determine what comes next.

For other patients, pleural disease may be associated with infection or inflammatory conditions, making accurate diagnosis equally important in guiding treatment.

Learning Through Real Clinical Practice

The Two-Day Pleuroscopy Camp combined patient care with hands-on learning.

For the healthcare professionals involved, the procedure room became a space for practical exchange - where techniques could be observed, discussed, and applied in a real clinical environment.

Pleural procedures require more than technical familiarity. They involve patient assessment, imaging interpretation, procedural planning, equipment preparation, infection prevention, specimen handling, and close monitoring throughout and after the intervention.

Working alongside experienced colleagues provided opportunities to appreciate these different components as part of one continuous process.

The camp also reinforced the importance of ultrasound-guided pleural care, which can assist clinicians in identifying pleural fluid and planning procedures more safely and precisely.

Bringing these elements together helps create a more complete approach to pleural disease - one that considers not only the procedure, but the patient before, during, and after it.

Teamwork Behind Every Procedure

A successful pleural procedure is rarely the work of one person.

Behind the clinician performing the procedure is a wider team preparing equipment, supporting the patient, monitoring their condition, handling specimens, maintaining safety standards, and ensuring appropriate follow-up.

The camp highlighted the importance of this multidisciplinary approach.

Clinicians and nurses worked alongside support teams to coordinate different stages of patient care. Such collaboration is especially important when working with procedures that require careful preparation and monitoring.

For the patient, this teamwork may not always be visible. But it is an important part of ensuring that care is organized, safe, and responsive to individual needs.

From Diagnosis to the Next Step

A diagnostic procedure only becomes meaningful when its findings can be used to guide patient care.

The samples and observations obtained during pleuroscopy can contribute to establishing a diagnosis, helping clinicians determine the most appropriate next steps.

For a patient with an unexplained pleural effusion, this may mean finally understanding why the fluid has accumulated. For someone with suspected malignancy, it may provide the evidence needed to move towards definitive diagnosis and treatment planning.

In this sense, the impact of the camp goes beyond the procedure room.

It lies in helping shorten the distance between a patient's first unanswered question and the care that follows.

Putting the Patient First

The Osler quote at the heart of this story speaks to something fundamental about medicine: patients are more than the diseases they carry.

A pleural effusion is a clinical finding, but behind it is a person who may be struggling to breathe, worried about what the symptoms mean, or anxious about what a possible diagnosis could bring.

Specialized procedures should therefore be considered not only in terms of technical achievement, but in terms of what they make possible for that person.

A more precise diagnosis can reduce uncertainty.

A timely intervention can relieve symptoms or guide treatment.

Clear communication can help a patient understand what is happening.

And careful follow-up can ensure that care continues after the procedure itself.

This patient-centred approach is essential to improving the experience and outcomes of people living with respiratory disease.

Growing Expertise Through Shared Experience

The Two-Day Pleuroscopy Camp also provided an opportunity for healthcare professionals to deepen their practical experience in pleural medicine.

Specialized respiratory procedures continue to evolve, and ongoing learning is an important part of maintaining high standards of care. Creating opportunities for clinicians to learn together allows knowledge and experience to move between teams and institutions.

For ReSoK, this aligns with its commitment to improving lung health through education, training, research, and technical support.

Such initiatives create a link between professional development and patient care. What is learned during a procedure today can influence how another patient is assessed tomorrow.

Over time, that exchange of knowledge can help create a stronger and more connected respiratory community.

Looking Beyond the Camp

The significance of the Two-Day Pleuroscopy Camp is not limited to the patients seen or procedures performed during the two days.

Its wider value lies in the relationships built, the knowledge exchanged, and the experience carried forward by the healthcare professionals involved.

It demonstrates what can happen when clinical expertise and teamwork are brought together around a specific patient need.

For ReSoK, collaboration remains an important part of advancing lung health in Kenya and beyond. Working alongside healthcare professionals, institutions, partners, and support teams creates opportunities to bring specialized knowledge closer to the communities that need it.

Pleural disease may begin with a symptom that seems difficult to explain. But with the right expertise, appropriate technology, and a coordinated approach to care, that uncertainty can begin to give way to answers.

And behind every procedure, every sample, and every diagnosis is the person who matters most - the patient.
